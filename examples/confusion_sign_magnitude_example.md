# Confusion Example: Sign vs Magnitude

Paused location: derivation line moving from

\[
a+b=c
\]

to

\[
a=c-b
\]

Direct answer: the sign of \(b\) changes because \(b\) is subtracted from both sides, not because its magnitude changes.

Missing dependency: equality-preserving operations.

Minimal example:

\[
3+2=5
\]

Subtract \(2\) from both sides:

\[
3=5-2
\]

The magnitude \(2\) is unchanged; its sign is negative on the right because it was moved by subtraction.

Reconnection: when the paper moves a regularization term across the equality, the same operation causes the sign flip.

Resume point: return to the derivation line after the moved term.
